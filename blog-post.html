Why approval gates matter more than model accuracy

Dara Okonkwo
12 min read · 18 Jul 2026

Every few months, a new model comes out that's a little more accurate, a little faster, a little cheaper. It's tempting to think that's the whole game — that once the model is good enough, operating teams will trust it to just run.

In practice, that's not what we see. The teams that successfully hand off real operational work to AI agents aren't the ones with the most accurate models. They're the ones with the clearest approval gates.

Accuracy isn't the same as trust

An agent that's right 95% of the time is still wrong one time in twenty. For low-stakes actions, that's a fine trade-off. For a refund, a permission change, or an email sent on your company's behalf, it's not — no matter how good the model is.

What actually builds trust is knowing that the 5% of cases where the agent isn't confident, or where the action is sensitive, get routed to a human before anything happens.

Designing gates that don't slow you down

The trick is being selective. If everything requires approval, you haven't automated anything — you've just added a review step. We recommend starting with a short list of clearly sensitive actions (refunds over a threshold, permission grants, external communications) and expanding automation as your agents build a track record.

Over time, as your agents demonstrate consistent, correct behavior on a given action type, you can loosen the gate — or remove it entirely.

The real unlock

Approval gates aren't a workaround for imperfect models. They're the mechanism that lets operating teams comfortably expand what they automate over time, because every decision — approved or not — is visible, logged, and reversible. That transparency, more than any accuracy benchmark, is what actually lets teams say yes to automation.
